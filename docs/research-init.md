# Initial Research Briefing: Clover Health Member-Facing Chatbot Case Study

## TL;DR

- **Build a citation-required, RAG-grounded, senior-first member assistant that answers ONLY from public plan documents (Evidence of Coverage, Summary of Benefits, formulary, provider directory) in v1 - no PHI, no authentication - and escalates everything else to a human.** This sidesteps HIPAA/BAA scope and CMS marketing-material review while still deflecting the highest-volume "how does my plan work" calls. Frame it explicitly as a CMS "communications" tool, not "marketing," and emphatically _not_ a coverage-determination tool (CMS's AI scrutiny targets the latter).
- **Anchor the business case on defensible numbers:** ~$5–$9 per health-plan call, ~6.6-minute average handle time, and a realistic 25–40% blended deflection ceiling for healthcare/insurance self-service. Cite UnitedHealthcare's "Avery" (live to ~160,000 Medicare Advantage members; 90% of the time members needed no advocate) as the closest MA proof point, and Juniper Research's $0.50–$0.70 saved per interaction as the analyst anchor.

### 2. Medicare Advantage domain rules that shape the build

- **CMS "communications" vs. "marketing" (42 CFR 422.2260–2274):** _Communications_ = any activity/material providing information to current/prospective enrollees (and their caregivers). _Marketing_ is a subset intended to draw attention to a plan and influence enrollment/plan selection. **A member-support chatbot answering benefits questions is "communications," not "marketing"** - keeping you out of the strictest HPMS material-review / pre-approval regime (which centers on marketing materials, scripts, general-audience ads, etc.). Caveat: certain communications "critical to beneficiaries understanding or accessing benefits" (e.g., the EOC) still get CMS review, and CMS can require review of _any_ material based on complaints/data.
- **CMS AI rules:** The CY2026 Proposed Rule (Dec 10, 2024) floated AI "guardrails" requiring MA plans to use AI "in a manner that preserves equitable access to MA services" (broadly defining "automated systems"), but CMS **declined to finalize** them in the April 4, 2025 Final Rule. Binding guidance therefore remains the **CY2024 Final Rule (CMS-4201-F) + the Feb 6, 2024 FAQ**: AI/algorithms may _assist_ but must not _make_ coverage denials; determinations must be individualized; AI "should not function to delay or discourage care." **Takeaway: keep the bot far from coverage/authorization/denial decisions - it informs, it does not adjudicate.** This is also a live litigation area (class actions against MA insurers over algorithmic denials), so the "informs-not-adjudicates" boundary is both a legal and a narrative asset.
- **HIPAA:** No LLM is "HIPAA-compliant" on its own. If PHI is involved you need (1) a **BAA-eligible tier** - OpenAI API/Enterprise, Anthropic API/enterprise, Azure OpenAI, AWS Bedrock, Google Vertex (never consumer ChatGPT/Claude.ai tiers); (2) zero-data-retention configuration; (3) minimum-necessary + RBAC; (4) audit logging; (5) encryption. The BAA covers the vendor's side only - the covered entity still owns audit/disclosure. **The clean design for a 40-hour build: v1 handles zero PHI** - answer only from public documents; require auth + PHI only in a documented "v2" roadmap. Use a BAA-eligible model tier even in v1 as a hygiene signal.
- **Public vs. authenticated content:**
  - _Safe without auth (v1):_ EOC, Summary of Benefits, formulary/drug tiers, provider/pharmacy directory, OTC/supplemental benefit descriptions, premiums, the general grievance/appeal _process_, ID-card _how-to_.
  - _Requires auth + PHI (v2):_ claim status/EOB, deductible/accumulator balances, prior-auth status, the member's own medication history, ID-card reissue to an address.

### 3. Call-center economics (the ROI spine)

- **Cost per health-plan call:** ~$4.90–$8.50 (healthcare); insurance-specific ~$6.60. These are **vendor/BPO benchmarks (Gitnux, Confido, Dialog Health, GigaBPO, Nextiva) - directional, not analyst-grade.** Use a $5–$9 range and label it.
- **AHT:** **~6.6 minutes** for healthcare contact centers (a figure the vendor SpinSci attributes to National Library of Medicine research); **7–10+ minutes** for insurance/coverage calls (Nextiva). MA calls skew longer (elderly members, benefits complexity).
- **Realistic deflection:** **25–45% ceiling** for healthcare/insurance self-service (compliance + identity verification push contacts to humans). Be skeptical of vendor "85%" claims - those are usually per-_workflow_ containment, not blended end-to-end deflection (Prosper AI's own framing: 70% containment on scheduling ≈ 21–28% end-to-end when scheduling is only 30–40% of the mix).
- **Per-interaction savings anchor (analyst):** Per **Juniper Research's "Chatbots: Retail, eCommerce, Banking & Healthcare 2017-2022" (author Lauren Foye, May 2017): "healthcare and banking providers using bots can expect average time savings of just over 4 minutes per enquiry, equating to average cost savings in the range of $0.50-$0.70 per interaction"** - flag as a 2017-vintage forecast. **Gartner** (Aug 2022, forward projection): conversational AI to reduce agent labor costs by $80B in 2026, with labor up to 95% of contact-center cost; Gartner also cautions GenAI cost/resolution may exceed $3 by 2030, so model realistic per-resolution run costs (~$1–$3), not just labor avoidance.
- **Payer proof points:** **UnitedHealthcare "Avery"** - per the UnitedHealth Group newsroom (Mar 26, 2026): _"While 90% of the time, members who use Avery have not required assistance from an advocate, Avery will automatically transfer members to advocates when a live call is needed or requested"_; live to ~6.5M employer-sponsored + **160,000 MA members**, targeting 20.5M by end of 2026; 65M+ AI-first-answered calls in 2024. **Anthem/Elevance "Sydney Health"** - 12M+ registered members (2022, +40% YoY) but no published deflection %; app-store reviews describe its chat as a slow search box. **Humana/IBM Watson Assistant** - 1.3M employer medical + 1.8M dental members (2021) but no published containment number.

### 4. Competitive landscape

- **Payer assistants:** **Alignment Healthcare's AVA** (160+ AI models, 200+ data sources, 13,000+ attributes; but it's a care-coordination/clinical-stratification platform, not a member self-service chatbot); **Anthem/Elevance Sydney Health** (basic); **UnitedHealthcare Avery** (the most advanced MA-facing GenAI companion). Most payer "chatbots" underdeliver on true conversational resolution - a differentiation opportunity.
- **Healthcare conversational-AI vendors (self-reported metrics - treat as vendor marketing):** Hyro (85%+ deflection at health _systems_; Intermountain 85% deflection/99% hold-time reduction; Baptist Health 79% deflection on IT/MyChart-password calls, ~$1M/90-day savings); Cognigy (unnamed Fortune 100 insurer: 20M calls/yr, 95% automation of ID&V, AHT −1.5 min); plus Notable, Talkdesk Healthcare, Assort Health, Infinitus (payer phone calls), Hippocratic AI (clinical), Avaamo (HIPAA-compliant). **Common failure modes to call out:** per-workflow (not end-to-end) containment, poor senior usability, weak escalation, and hallucination on benefit specifics.

### 5. Senior (65+) UX - the real differentiator

- **Nielsen Norman Group** ("UX Design for Seniors, 3rd ed.," 87 guidelines): older users struggle with tiny fonts, small/clustered targets, dropdowns/sliders (hard with declining motor skills on touch), hover states, and unintuitive icons. Recommendations: **≥16px base font** (NN/g's older guidance says ≥12pt default minimum; 16px is the safer modern floor), generous white space between links to reduce mis-taps, **persistent visible navigation** (avoid hamburger menus for primary nav), and plain-language error recovery.
- **WCAG 2.2 AA (new criteria most relevant here):** 2.5.8 **Target Size (Minimum) 24×24 CSS px** - but use the AAA 2.5.5 **44×44** for a senior audience; 2.4.11 **Focus Not Obscured**; 2.4.13 **Focus Appearance** (≥2px perimeter, 3:1 contrast between focused/unfocused); 3.2.6 **Consistent Help**; 3.3.8 **Accessible Authentication** (no cognitive-test-only auth - allow paste/passkeys); 3.3.7 **Redundant Entry**. Section 508 (which incorporates WCAG) applies to Medicare-related digital products.
- **Senior tech reality:** 76% of 65+ own a smartphone (2024 Pew analysis); ~30% of adults 65+ never use the internet (older Pew data put "never online" near 25–33%, sharply higher for 80+). Per **AARP "2026 Tech Trends and Adults 50-Plus" (Kakulla, Dec 2025, fielded Sept 9–Oct 6, 2025): AI usage "nearly doubled, up from 18% in 2024 to 30% in 2025"** (9% in 2023). But the **University of Michigan National Poll on Healthy Aging (2025)** found 55% of adults 50+ have used AI they speak or type to, yet **46% have little or no trust in it.** **Design implication: voice-first, big-target, high-contrast, radically simple, an always-visible "Talk to a person" button, and explicit trust cues (visible source citations; "I'm an assistant, not a doctor; I only use your plan documents").**

### 6. Technical architecture

- **RAG (2026 best practice):** Per **Anthropic's "Contextual Retrieval in AI Systems" (Sept 2024): Contextual Embeddings cut top-20-chunk retrieval failure ~35% (5.7%→3.7%); adding Contextual BM25 → ~49% (→2.9%); adding reranking → ~67% (→1.9%).** Prepend a 50–100-token chunk-specific context before embedding + BM25 indexing (use prompt caching to keep contextualization cheap). Use semantic/header-based chunking (`MarkdownHeaderTextSplitter` on `##`/`###`), hybrid dense+BM25 fused via **Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF, k=60, ~0.7 dense / 0.3 lexical weights)**, a reranker (Cohere Rerank / Voyage / a cross-encoder), and query rewriting/HyDE. Evaluate with RAGAS / DeepEval / Braintrust (target faithfulness ≥0.9).
- **Supabase pgvector:** **HNSW is the 2026 default** (better recall/speed tradeoff, no training pass, handles inserts; ~<10ms p99 up to ~5M vectors on a Supabase Pro instance) over IVFFlat (faster build, less memory, needs periodic rebuilds, only for static/memory-constrained data). Build with `CREATE INDEX ON docs USING hnsw (embedding vector_cosine_ops);`. Hybrid search = `tsvector` (GIN index) + pgvector HNSW, fused via RRF inside a Postgres **RPC function** (Supabase publishes a canonical `hybrid_search` RPC). Use **Row-Level Security** for any future multi-tenant/member data.
- **Voice-first latency:** Per ElevenLabs docs, **"Eleven Flash v2.5…delivers high-quality speech with ultra-low latency (~75ms†) across 32 languages"**, sub-500ms end-to-end, model id **`eleven_flash_v2_5`**, ~50% lower price/character than Multilingual v2; supports streaming/WebSocket. **Fish Audio** as fallback. STT options: Deepgram, Whisper, ElevenLabs Scribe v2, or the browser **Web Speech API** (free but weaker on older/accented voices - flag ASR-accuracy risk for seniors and default to text with voice as opt-in if it tests poorly). End-to-end time-to-first-audio = STT + LLM TTFT + TTS + network; optimize with token streaming, parallel/speculative retrieval, and Vercel edge functions.
- **Semantic caching:** GPTCache / Redis semantic cache / Upstash / Vercel KV; cache on embedding similarity **above a high threshold (~0.95+)** for health answers to avoid wrong-answer collisions; invalidate on any document update. Big win for repeated "what's my copay for X" FAQs.
- **Guardrails:** citation-required answering (refuse if no supporting chunk clears the confidence threshold), explicit escalation/human-handoff, hallucination mitigation, "I don't know - let me connect you" fallback, and out-of-scope refusal for anything clinical or coverage-determination-related.
- **Web scraping cloverhealth.com / investors.cloverhealth.com:** **Firecrawl** (hosted, clean markdown, handles PDFs/SEC filings, excellent DX, MCP server - but **AGPL**, flag for legal if commercialized) or **Crawl4AI** (open-source **Apache 2.0**, self-hostable, free, "Scrapy for LLMs," async). **Recommendation: Crawl4AI for the bulk + Firecrawl for tough pages; MarkItDown for local PDF→markdown of 10-K/EOC/SB PDFs.** Respect robots.txt and ToS; scraping a company's own public site for an interview project is low-risk, but document that you honored robots.txt and used public pages only.
